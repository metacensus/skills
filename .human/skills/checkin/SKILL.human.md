A pre-flight against drift a build misses: reformatting, duplicated derived state, reinvented conventions.

## 1. Branch — never the default branch

`<your-name>/<topic>`, naming the owner and the change's subject.

## 2. Match precedent before you invent

The highest-value habit: copy siblings' shape key by key; schema-valid is not conventional.

## 3. Never author derived state

No computed facts or claims a later edit would falsify.

## 4. Check what else your edit made false

Re-read siblings: their prose can turn false while everything passes.

## 5. Keep the diff surgical

Only the lines the change needs.

A proposal on someone's branch carries only the proposal; a rebase goes on a branch of its own.

## 6. Run the gate

Locally too.

## 7. Ask what settles what you altered

Obey `check-cells.mts --diff`, the path-to-review map, even when sure; authors judge their own work worst.

In a public repository, the `open-source` review too.

## 8. Read your own diff

As a reviewer: proportional, nothing moved, no stray files.

## 9. Commit and PR text

Observed style, elegance cells 5C and 4C, attribution.

## 10. Stop at a verified diff

Committing is the user's call, unless they handed over the branch.
