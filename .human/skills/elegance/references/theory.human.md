The argument the rest of the skill rests on: how a project's documents and code drift from reality, and how to keep them corrected.

## 1 · Reality and perception

A project holds a partial, delayed representation of reality. Elegance is one objective seen from two sides: clarity (acting on it lands where intended) and fewest elements (each can drift and must be kept in agreement). Abstractness is how long reality takes to reveal an element as wrong — minutes for a file, a decade for a principle — and that becomes the scope ladder. Completeness only matters where a missing fact would change an action.

## 2 · Information and action

Information drives action and action produces new information, in a loop of future, action, past, and current. Only observation and action touch reality; everything else is inference, where most drift enters. An element's time is its station in that loop, not its grammatical tense. The code, schema, and data are the primary elements; a comment is a second element that must earn its place. A reference beats a copy, a duplicate is a missing link, and anything computable — counts, lists, claims like "the only" — should be computed, not written.

## 3 · Drift

Drift is the growing gap between what is written and what is true, as either wrong content or missing content. Checks catch wrongness only, never gaps, so open questions must be marked as holes. Drift compounds through inheritance, neglect, and leftover traces of old versions; misplaced detail compounds fastest. Risk is weighted by how much depends on an element and how weak its check is; agent-written content starts at the weakest level, however fluent.

## 4 · Alignment

Alignment is continual correction, so expand only as fast as you can check. Prefer carrying a fact in the artifact or a gate over a sentence; summarize upward, reference sideways, never explain downward. Agents are biased toward adding, so restraint needs explicit artifacts — non-goals, what was left alone. A change is finished when nothing describes what it replaced.
