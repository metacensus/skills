> A proposal puts one binary question to those with standing; only their agreement raises it above assertion.

```
            1A vision
                ↓
0A proposal ↔ 0B rules ↔ 0C retired rules

Proposal → Decision ─ yes → into 0B; what it replaces → 0C
                    └ no  → 0C with the reason
```

Tier 0 · scope Principles · time Future

## Example

A proposed writing rule: the case for it · the alternatives rejected · who has to say yes.

## Location

**Proposals**, in the location key (grid).

## Clear when

The 0B rule it would alter is named · the question is binary · the rejected alternatives are present · agreement by those with standing.

## Excess

| Excess | Test or move |
|---|---|
| **The case argued at essay length** | Keep the question, the rule it changes, the cost of each answer. Cut the rest; if they can still agree, it was delay. |
| **Alternatives described rather than listed** | One line each, with why not. |
| **A proposal that also drafts the handbook page** | The rule lands in 0B by decision; drafting here duplicates the page before it exists. |
| **A proposal no one still wants answered, left open** | A shadow rule, followed inconsistently; retire it to 0C. |

## Cost

Low until adopted · then the cost of 0B.

## Moves

An agent can sharpen, never decide.

| When | Then |
|---|---|
| Question unclear, or no one wants it | Sharpen it, or retire the proposal |
| Yes | Moves into 0B; what it replaces retires to 0C |
| No | Retires to 0C with the reason |
