**When a person says the work is anchored, the fix is the problem, not another option: every option offered from inside the anchor is a variant of it.**

## Stop

Drop the earlier solution as the frame. No patch, defence, or variant; one line saying you are stepping back. Nothing it built is undone yet.

## Restate the problem

Who has it, what they need, what is held fixed, and what done looks like, in no term the earlier solution coined. End by asking the person to correct it.

## Check precedent

- **Sibling apps:** the person's or organization's other projects that met the same need.
- **Best practice:** the platform's or framework's documented way, and widely used tools.

"Isn't that the whole point of X" names the first precedent: read what X does before anything else. Cite each by link or path, with one line on what it does, and say "none found" where none is. This widens [`checkin`'s precedent](../checkin/SKILL.md#2-match-precedent-before-you-invent) from a record's shape to the problem.

## Agree the foundations

The foundations are the restated problem, the constraints, and what the precedents teach. Until the person agrees them, in their own words or by confirming the restatement, ask open questions only: no option menu, no choice buttons, no recommendation. Silence is not agreement.

## Rebuild

Options return, each grounded in a precedent. The earlier solution is one candidate, judged against the restated problem, not the yardstick the others are measured against.
